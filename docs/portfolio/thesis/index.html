Theoretical Foundations of Neural Networks and PINNs

An undergraduate thesis on why physics-informed neural networks fail, located at three levels — approximation, optimisation, and the stability of the problem itself — and a PyTorch implementation that tests each claim with an experiment whose pass/fail criterion was fixed before it ran.

01

The Argument

A PINN represents the solution of a differential problem by a network uθ and minimises the residual of the equation at a set of collocation points. On problems with a small length scale it degrades, and the usual accounts treat each failure as an anecdote. The thesis separates three independent causes, each at a different level:

  • Approximation — spectral bias. Which frequencies a network learns first, and whether the differential operator changes that order.
  • Optimisation — gradient pathology. The residual and boundary terms of the loss pull with gradients of very different size.
  • Stability — of the problem itself. Whether a small residual implies a small error at all, and under which hypothesis.

The distinctive choice is to study the network through derivatives with respect to its input rather than its parameters — the opposite of general deep-learning texts, and the right one here, because the operator differentiates in x. Explicit recurrences for the Jacobian and Hessian follow, and from them a necessary condition on the activation function. That condition is what makes the ReLU case sharp: under a second-order operator the residual objective is not merely hard to optimise, it is locally constant.

The Mathematics, in the Thesis's Notation

Four results the rest of the work stands on, written as the thesis writes them.

The network

An L-layer feed-forward network with a linear output layer, defined recursively; σ acts componentwise.

a(0)=x

z(ℓ)=W(ℓ)a(ℓ−1)+b(ℓ),ℓ=1,…,L

a(ℓ)=σ(z(ℓ)),ℓ=1,…,L−1

Netθ(x)=z(L)

Backpropagation: four equations

With the local error δ(ℓ)≔∂J/∂z(ℓ), σ ∈ C¹ and a differentiable loss J, one backward sweep gives every parameter gradient. The hero figure on the home page draws exactly this sweep.

The loss of a PINN

Three mean-square terms — the residual at Nr collocation points, the initial condition, the boundary operator 𝓑 — combined with positive weights; 𝓛 is the differential operator with coefficients ζ. Training is unconstrained minimisation of J.

J(θ)=λrJr(θ)+λ0J0(θ)+λbJb(θ)

Jr(θ)=1Nr∑i=1Nr|rθ(xri,tri)|2,rθ=∂tuθ+𝓛[uθ;ζ]

Why minimising a residual is worth doing

The method minimises ‖rθ‖, but what matters is the error eθ = uθ − u, which cannot be computed. For −u″ = f on (0, 1) with u(0) = u(1) = 0, and a network that satisfies the boundary condition exactly:

‖eθ′‖L2≤1π‖rθ‖L2,‖eθ‖L2≤1π2‖rθ‖L2

Chapter 4, §4.1 — proposition, one-dimensional Poisson problem

Proof. Subtracting the equations, −eθ″ = rθ with eθ(0) = eθ(1) = 0. Multiply by eθ, integrate by parts (the boundary terms vanish), then apply Cauchy–Schwarz and the Poincaré inequality, whose constant 1/π is optimal — attained by sin πx, the eigenfunction of the smallest eigenvalue π² of −d²/dx²:

‖eθ′‖2=∫01rθeθdx≤‖rθ‖‖eθ‖≤1π‖rθ‖‖eθ′‖

Divide by ‖eθ′‖ for the first bound; apply Poincaré once more for the second. ∎ The constant is favourable — the residual shrinks by 1/π² ≈ 0.101 on its way to the error — but only under the hypothesis. Experiment 6 measures what happens when that hypothesis is weakened.

What the results changed

A retracted claim. Experiment 3 contradicted the thesis's own chapter 4, which had concluded that a PINN is least accurate at exactly the frequencies it learns slowest. The effective spectrum has two competing factors; the chapter had argued each separately and then assumed the second one wins. In the moderate-frequency band the first one does. The published text keeps the experiment and retracts the claim; the proved propositions stand. The figure at the top of this page is that experiment.

A lemma from a failure. On Burgers the standard fix — adaptive weight annealing — made the error four times worse. Rather than omit it, the thesis proves why: the annealing rule has a positive-feedback regime. That lemma exists only because the experiment failed.

05

Implementation

The method is written directly on PyTorch's automatic differentiation, so that nothing the argument depends on is hidden inside a library. core.py (150 lines) holds the network with optional Fourier features, derivatives of any order, Adam and L-BFGS, and gradient-imbalance diagnostics; each experiment is one script, and run_all.py reaches all six.

The central primitive is four lines of code. It is the input-derivative recurrences of chapter 3, and create_graph=True is what makes the result itself differentiable in θ — the loss contains ∂xxuθ, and training needs its gradient, so reverse mode runs through reverse mode:

# code/pinns/core.py (docstring omitted)
def grad(y: torch.Tensor, x: torch.Tensor, order: int = 1) -> torch.Tensor:
    for _ in range(order):
        y = torch.autograd.grad(y, x, torch.ones_like(y), create_graph=True)[0]
    return y

This is also exactly where ReLU breaks: with σ″ ≡ 0, the second call returns structural zeros, the residual loss no longer depends on θ at the collocation points, and every gradient-based optimiser is stationary — experiment 1.

  • make test — 20 unit tests of the primitives against closed-form mathematics.
  • make verify — re-derives the 39 published numbers from code/results/*.json, in seconds; CI runs it.
  • make experiments — re-runs all six experiments and rewrites the results.

06

Reproducibility, Measured

Every experiment pins a seed and runs in float64. That is not enough for digit-for-digit reproduction, and the first attempt to say how far it goes was wrong: measured on one machine at 1, 2 and 4 threads, it passed; on GitHub's runners — a different CPU with different SIMD kernels — three quantities disagreed. What survived is a smaller, sharper claim:

QuantityReproducesWhy
ReLU's uxx and ∇Jr; the parameter countbit-for-bit, any machinenot sums at all: σ″ ≡ 0 makes them structural zeros; a count is an integer
One forward/backward passto about 1 ulpfloating point, with no trajectory to accumulate along
tanh quantities after 4,000 Adam stepsno — up to 3.4%epsilon-sized differences amplified along the optimisation trajectory

So the verification does not diff the results files, which would look rigorous and fail on any machine. It checks each of the 39 items at the precision it supports — closed-form identities to machine precision, internal consistency, inequalities and signs, and a real re-run of experiment 1 — and was itself mutation-tested: setting the fitted exponent to the "expected" −1 is caught.

07

Scope and Authorship

  • Mine: the theory, the proofs and the argument of the thesis.
  • AI-assisted, and marked as such in the commit history: editing (compile errors, captions, bibliography, tightening several proofs, unifying notation in chapters 2–3), re-running experiments 3–5 with the configurations the text specifies, and later the test suite, the verification script, the figures, packaging and CI.
  • Not claimed: an autodiff engine written from scratch. The thesis derives forward and reverse mode; the implementation is built on torch.autograd.

The thesis is written in Vietnamese. A compiled PDF is not published yet — the repository builds one with make thesis (XeLaTeX).

FlagshipScientific MLThesis

Theoretical Foundations of Neural Networks and PINNs

Problem
Physics-informed networks degrade on problems with a small length scale. Why — and at which level: approximation, optimisation, or the differential problem itself?
Method
Feed-forward networks studied through derivatives with respect to the input; backpropagation and forward/reverse-mode differentiation derived; gradient pathology, spectral bias and a stability bound analysed.
Built
A PINN written directly on torch.autograd — 885 lines, no PINN library; 20 tests against closed-form mathematics and 39 checks that re-derive every published number in CI.
Result
Six experiments with criteria fixed in advance. Under ReLU the residual objective is exactly stationary; the operator reverses spectral bias; Burgers at small viscosity is reported as a negative result.

05 / About

About

Mathematics and Computer Science at VNU-HCM University of Science, graduating October 2027. The work sits where mathematics meets code: neural networks studied through their derivatives, numerical methods whose stability claims become tests, and quantitative research designed so that the answer is allowed to be no.

Probability, statistics and linear algebra carry the most weight — in evaluation design, in what a metric actually measures, and in knowing when a difference between two models is not a difference. The numerical analysis comes from three courses whose reports and code are on this site. I completed the FlyRank machine-learning internship track; the ranking system above was its capstone.

How I work — the same method in every project

  1. State the claim precisely — a decision and its metric, a rate, a stability bound — before building anything. ranking, PPA
  2. Derive what should happen, then measure against ground truth: an exact Riemann solver, an analytic threshold, a closed-form identity. Euler, FEM
  3. Beat a transparent baseline first — a rule, a buy-and-hold, a single retriever — and remove anything that leaks the answer. ranking, quant, RAG
  4. Hard-code no conclusions. Make the textbook claim a test the build has to pass. LMM
  5. Let CI re-derive the published numbers, so a report and its code cannot drift apart. thesis, FEM
  6. Publish what failed next to what worked: a negative result, a retracted claim, a discarded model. thesis, ranking
